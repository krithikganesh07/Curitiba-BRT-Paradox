Curitiba: The BRT Paradox

Why a city that pioneered Bus Rapid Transit is struggling to keep its passengers.

A public transport research project exploring the challenges, performance and future of Curitiba's Bus Rapid Transit (BRT) system in Brazil.

1. Introduction

In 1974, Curitiba, Brazil, introduced a pioneering public transport model that would influence cities across the world.

Instead of relying entirely on expensive underground railways, Curitiba developed a system based on dedicated bus corridors, specialised stations, integrated routes and urban planning.

This became one of the world's most influential examples of Bus Rapid Transit (BRT).

But decades later, an important question emerges:

How can a city famous for its successful public transport model struggle with passenger numbers, congestion and service quality?

This project investigates the gap between Curitiba's international reputation and the challenges facing its transport system today.

The objective is not to declare Curitiba's BRT a failure. It is to understand its problems, evaluate the available evidence and explore realistic solutions.

---

2. Understanding Curitiba's BRT

Curitiba's transport system was designed around a simple principle:

Make buses operate more like a high-capacity rapid transit system.

Its main features include:

- Dedicated bus corridors.
- High-capacity articulated buses.
- Tube-shaped boarding stations.
- Integrated terminals and connecting routes.
- Fare integration.
- Urban development organised around major transport corridors.

The model demonstrated that effective urban mobility does not always require an underground railway.

However, maintaining such a system requires continuous investment, good operations and adaptation to changing travel patterns.

3. The Central Problem: The BRT Paradox

A transport system can be internationally recognised and still face serious operational challenges.

Some of the questions investigated in this project include:

Issue| Research question
Ridership| Are fewer people using the system than before?
Congestion| Are buses losing their speed advantage?
Infrastructure| Is the existing infrastructure sufficient?
Economics| Are operating and investment costs sustainable?
Accessibility| Can residents easily access the network?
Governance| Are transport services managed transparently?
Sustainability| Can electric buses and modernisation improve performance?

These are research questions, not predetermined conclusions.

8. Initial Evidence

Some important findings from the initial source review:

- Curitiba introduced its pioneering express bus system on 22 September 1974.
- The original system began with two express routes and approximately 20 km of dedicated corridors, according to the municipal government.
- The New Development Bank approved financing of up to US$75 million for a BRT improvement project in December 2020.
- The project identified reduced operating speeds and declining passenger numbers as challenges.
- Its expected targets included a 35% increase in average operating speed and a 25% reduction in travel time.

Important: These percentages are project targets, not verified achievements.

The research will distinguish between historical data, current figures and planned improvements.

9. Potential Solutions

The following are possible policy directions to investigate, not final recommendations.

1. Improve bus priority at intersections.
2. Increase service frequency where passenger demand justifies it.
3. Improve connections between neighbourhoods and major BRT corridors.
4. Modernise stations and terminals.
5. Introduce cleaner and more efficient buses.
6. Improve public reporting of transport performance.
7. Coordinate transport investment with urban development.

Every final recommendation should be supported by evidence and evaluated for cost, feasibility and social impact.

11. Limitations

Public transport statistics can describe different things.

For example, BRT corridor passengers, total bus passengers and total public transport passengers are not interchangeable.

This project will identify the scope, date and definition of each statistic before comparing figures.

It will also distinguish correlation from causation and avoid presenting proposed targets as achieved results.

13. Conclusion

Curitiba's BRT was once considered a revolutionary solution to urban transport challenges.

But transport systems cannot depend on past achievements alone.

Changing populations, urban expansion, congestion, financial pressures and evolving travel behaviour require continuous adaptation.

The real question is not whether Curitiba invented a successful transport model.

It is whether that model can continue to deliver affordable, reliable and accessible mobility in a changing city.

That is the Curitiba BRT Paradox.
